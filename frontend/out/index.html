How it works

Three steps from a prescription to a plan you can follow.

  1. Step 1

    Scan your prescription

    Take a photo. We read the medicine, dose and times, and you check every detail before anything is saved.

  2. Step 2

    See trials that might fit

    Your profile is checked against recruiting trials on ClinicalTrials.gov. Each result says why it was shown.

  3. Step 3

    Stay on track

    Your schedule builds itself. Mark doses as taken, and add an optional camera check-in when you want one.

Without PhaseZero

  • Search trial registries one condition at a time
  • Re-type the same details for every trial
  • Decode eligibility criteria written for researchers
  • Track doses and symptoms in a separate app or on paper

With PhaseZero

  • One profile, filled in once
  • Prescription details read from a photo
  • Every trial explained in plain English, with the reason it was shown
  • Doses, check-ins and trials on one dashboard

Why people take part in clinical trials

PhaseZero shows you trials that might fit. The trial team always makes the final decision with you.

  • Access to new treatments

    Some trials test treatments that are not yet widely available.

  • Extra care from a research team

    Trials often include regular check-ups and close monitoring.

  • Help people with your condition

    Every trial adds to what doctors know about treating it.

  • Your choice, always

    Taking part is voluntary. You can ask questions first and stop at any time.

Built for everyone who reads a medicine label

Clinical information is hard enough. The app shouldn't make it harder.

  • Easy-to-read text

    Set in Lexend, a typeface designed to reduce reading effort, at 16px or larger.

  • Plain English

    Medical terms are explained in everyday words. Nothing is hidden behind jargon.

  • Clear contrast

    Text meets WCAG AA contrast, and status is always shown with an icon and a word.

  • Big tap targets

    Every button is at least 44 pixels tall and works with a keyboard or screen reader.
